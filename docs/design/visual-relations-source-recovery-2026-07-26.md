## 1. Island / Domain Boundary

![Two diagonal bounding-node cuts across a wh-island tree](visual-relations-assets/source-recovery-2026-07-26/island-subjacency-cas-lx522-slide24.jpg)

- Source: *CAS LX 522 Syntax I: Week 12a. More wh-movement, Subjacency, and relative clauses*, Boston University teaching deck. The surviving mirrors do not expose a reliable author or original deck date; the SlideServe mirror was uploaded in 2014.
- Stable mirrors: https://slideplayer.com/slide/5088479/ and https://www.slideserve.com/harry/cas-lx-522-syntax-i
- Direct inspected image: https://image2.slideserve.com/4666474/wh-islands4-l.jpg
- Location: slide 24, "Wh-islands."
- Tree: illicit long operator movement across an embedded wh-question, with an occupied embedded Spec,CP.
- Visible mark: the orange movement trajectory crosses two equal diagonal red cuts, one at each IP bounding-node crossing.
- Licensed meaning: the slide states, "Subjacency. Operator movement cannot cross more than one bounding node" and identifies IP as a bounding node in English. The cuts therefore mark the two bounding nodes crossed in a single illicit movement step. They do not merely decorate or enclose an island.
- Convention status: an authentic syntactician-authored teaching visualization of a GB/Subjacency analysis. The diagonal cut is the exact source convention translated into Babel's accepted green barrier cuts.
- Reuse: the mirror states personal/informational use only; retain as a cited research plate, not a freely reusable production asset.
- Confidence: high that this is the exact plate used to design Babel's island UI.
- Limit: the Subjacency analysis and the choice of IP as a bounding node are theory-, language-, and period-specific. A different authored locality analysis may identify different boundaries; Babel must place the cuts from the relation's anchored boundary nodes rather than assume every island is IP.

## 2. Focus

![Focal nodes distinguished on a real syntax tree](visual-relations-assets/source-recovery-2026-07-26/focus-marked-nodes-assmann-et-al-2023-ex44.png)

- Source: Muriel Assmann, Daniel Buring, Izabela Jordanoska, Max Pruller et al., "Towards a theory of morphosyntactic focus marking" (2023), *Natural Language & Linguistic Theory* 41, 1349-1396.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11049-023-09567-4
- Location: journal page 1378, example/tree (44).
- Sentence: Hausa correction meaning "No, that the old man is beating the dog makes her angry."
- Visible mark: two focal nodes and their focus-marking morphemes are distinguished with two shades of blue on the syntax tree.
- Licensed meaning: the matrix subject clause is focal, and the embedded subject "the old man" is focal within it. The paper explicitly states that the colors distinguish those two focal nodes.
- Convention status: direct visual highlighting of focal nodes is transparent and linguistically licensed; the use of blue and the exact shades is author-specific.
- Reuse: CC BY 4.0.
- Confidence: high.
- Mismatch: example (44) validates node/subtree prominence, not branch weight. F-, G-, and CT-marking remain established alternatives when the analysis authors those distinctions.

### Correction 2026-07-26: the branch-strength precedent is example (49)

Example (44) is not the precedent for Babel's accepted focus drawing. The same
paper's example (49) is: English focus there is marked by metrical reversal, with
the focused branch promoted to strong and its demoted sister drawn as a dotted
weak branch. That is the accepted Babel design, one step of restyling away.

- Source: Assmann, Buring, Jordanoska and Pruller, "Towards a theory of morphosyntactic focus marking" (2023), *Natural Language & Linguistic Theory* 41, 1349-1396; DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s11049-023-09567-4.
- Location: example (49).
- Visible mark: promoted strong focus branch, dotted demoted sister.

## 3. Negative Polarity Answer / Sigma-to-Pol

![Sigma movement and elided TP in a French non response](visual-relations-assets/source-recovery-2026-07-26/polarity-sigma-pasquereau-2020-ex59.png)

- Source: Jérémy Pasquereau, "French polar response particles and neg movement" (2020), *Natural Language Semantics* 28, 255-306.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11050-020-09164-w
- Location: journal page 286, example (59b).
- Exchange: positive question "Did Marie finish?"; response "I think that she didn't."
- Visible mark: a lower Sigma occurrence inside the TP is connected by a curved arrow to an interpreted Sigma occurrence under a polarity head. The elided TP is boxed with a dash-dot boundary and muted.
- Licensed meaning: in this analysis, the lower negative Sigma head `Σᵢ−` moves to the response-particle head `Pol_rev`; only the highest Sigma copy is interpreted. The Pol head is pronounced as French `non`, while the recovered TP may be elided.
- Convention status: authentic and well-explained, but analysis-specific. Sigma is a real formal symbol in this paper, not a renderer-invented glyph. It should appear only when the model authors the corresponding polarity analysis.
- Reuse: CC BY 4.0.
- Confidence: high for this analysis, low as a universal polarity fallback.
- Mismatch: the figure simultaneously represents polarity movement, copy interpretation, and ellipsis. Babel should not infer all three from the word `Polarity`.

### Why Babel used Sigma

The accepted Babel plate copied the relation topology and notation from this exact figure. Pasquereau explicitly names the sentence-level polarity head `Σ`, values it positive or negative, requires Pol to agree with it, and then moves it to Pol. In example (59b), `Σᵢ−` is that negatively valued head: the lower occurrence belongs to the recovered proposition, and the higher occurrence is the interpreted copy under `Pol_rev`. The curved dependency and shared index display that Sigma chain.

This is **not** evidence that syntacticians use Sigma as a generic marker for negative-polarity-item licensing. The current plate is about a French bare negative response (`non`), polarity-head movement, copy interpretation, and TP ellipsis. Strong NPI licensing through an exhaustifier/Agree analysis is a separate relation; ordinary weak-NPI licensing often follows from semantic scope or downward entailment without this Sigma chain. Calling the current plate "NPI licensing" collapses distinct phenomena and is incorrect.

- Source: Ethan Poole and Stefan Keine, "Not all reconstruction effects are syntactic" (2024), *Natural Language & Linguistic Theory* 42, 1677-1725.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11049-023-09603-3
- Location: journal page 1678, example (2).
- Example: "someone from CA is likely to win the lottery."
- Visible mark: the lower copy is struck out for the surface-scope reading; the higher copy is struck out for the reconstructed-scope reading. The interpreted copy remains bold.
- Licensed meaning: strike-through marks the copy neglected at LF, while the unstruck copy is interpreted.
- Convention status: established copy-theoretic expository notation. Exact typography varies, but copy identity plus selective suppression is stable.
- Reuse: CC BY 4.0.
- Confidence: high.
- Mismatch: the source is a derivational representation rather than a full tree. It supports strike-through or selective ghosting of a complete coindexed copy, not arbitrary faded material and not a new movement arrow.

## Preliminary Composable-Vocabulary Observation

Across these four plates, a small composable vocabulary looks plausible, but this is a preliminary observation, not an inventory-wide conclusion. Each mark must remain relation-scoped. The marks are not a closed ontology and are not self-interpreting. These four examples do not license unrelated relations.

### Stable primitives

1. **Directed paths:** movement or a dependency when the source analysis licenses a direction. Line style alone cannot identify the relation.
2. **Indices and lineage:** coindex occurrences and copies. Renderer-generated display indices can identify a lineage without making the index an authored anchor.
3. **Selective suppression:** strike, muting, or ghosting for an unpronounced or uninterpreted occurrence. The exact state must come from authored structure, lineage, silence, or Replay.
4. **Node or subtree prominence:** color, weight, or highlight for focal material and other explicitly selected domains.
5. **Boundary marking:** cuts, circles, outlines, boxes, or shading anchored to the actual locality-bearing node, edge, or region.
6. **Feature displays:** compact matrices or valuation rows when the relevant values are authored in current node or Replay data.
7. **Judgment marks:** `*`, `?`, `#`, and related diagnostics when the model explicitly authors the judgment.

### Where conventions conflict

- Arrows are used for movement, binding, control, Agree, and semantic dependencies in different sources.
- Boxes and shading can mean an island/domain, ellipsis, a phase complement, or merely the region under discussion.
- Dashing can mean covert movement, a weak dependency, an omitted branch, or graphic de-emphasis.
- Color has no stable universal semantics.
- Sigma/Pol, F/G/CT, and similar labels are theory-bearing authored content, not generic renderer categories.

Therefore Babel should compose a small number of geometric primitives while dispatching them from open relation semantics plus anchored evidence. It should not map every unknown relation to an arbitrary generic arrow, box, or glow.

### Prominent relations still merit bespoke visuals

The accepted baseline remains justified for prominent relation families whose conventional combinations carry distinct meaning: movement trajectories, control, Agree/feature valuation, locality/islands, ellipsis, multidominance, PF realization, and LF/polarity. Composability should reduce duplicated drawing machinery, not flatten those designs into one generic appearance.

## Implications for These Four Plates

1. The island source directly supports the accepted diagonal boundary cuts. Each cut must be anchored to an authored bounding-node crossing; an unanchored slash is not enough.
2. Focus has source support for prominence marking in two forms: node/subtree highlighting in example (44), and promoted-strong/dotted-weak branch marking in example (49). Example (49) is the precedent for Babel's accepted branch-strength design. Neither licenses any association claim.
3. The Sigma polarity drawing is legitimate only when the authored analysis supplies the corresponding Sigma/Pol response analysis or equivalent evidence. It must not be synthesized for arbitrary negation or generic NPI licensing.
4. Reconstruction should operate over complete coindexed copies. The strongest recovered visual rule is selective copy suppression; it does not require a separate reconstruction trajectory.

5. decide whether Babel should compose existing primitives, create a bespoke drawing, or add no overlay because the authored tree or Replay already expresses the relation;
