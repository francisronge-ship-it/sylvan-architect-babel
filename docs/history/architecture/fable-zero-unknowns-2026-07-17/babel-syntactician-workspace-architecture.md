## 0. Executive conclusion

The Babel TreeBank should become what Francis has now settled: **a searchable library of complete Babel analyses, with collections and a syntactic research-notebook layer** — the human-facing research workspace over Babel's canonical derivational database, one workspace for everyone, organized around the **Saved Babel Work**: the outcome of one "Save to TreeBank" action, which canonically is an ordered set of per-analysis W17 records (one per generated analysis, grouped by `bundleId`, ordered by `analysisIndex`) under a thin wrapper of references, selection, configuration, and view state — the thing Babel actually makes and restores — rather than the *tree* as a picture.

Three findings drive everything else:

1. **The derivational-treebank problem is solved in principle, and the solution is the one Babel's architecture already adopted on paper.** DELPH-IN Redwoods, the LFG Parsebanker/INESS, and (statically) CCGbank and MGbank all store *derivations relative to a grammar version* as the primary record and *re-project* derived trees, AVMs, and semantics from them; Redwoods' discriminant-replay makes annotation decisions survive grammar revision. Babel's canonical architecture already cites Redwoods for exactly this posture ("views re-projectable under newer engines"). What exists in code today is the **record-contract half only**: the W17a–d chain (durable envelope → evidence schemas → evidence-bound join → canonical native-JSON export) is an explicitly **persistence-free** validation/construction/export layer — its README says so, and no W17 module writes to any store. **Durable storage, record lookup, and canonical-database ownership are architecture-only and unbuilt.** The workspace's job is to be the human face of that future record system — never a second one — and Tranche 1's job is to build the first real persistence behind the existing contracts. **[EVIDENCE]** for the treebank precedent; **[CONSEQUENCE]** for the build obligation.
2. **None of the surveyed tools serves the theoretical syntactician's own working life.** Corpus annotation platforms (TrEd, INCEpTION, Arborator-Grew) are annotation factories for teams building corpora; teaching tools (TreeForm, jsSyntaxTree) produce pictures, not data; the surveyed query tools cannot express derivation steps; and the research sweep found *no published study or tool* addressing how theoretical syntacticians manage their personal collections of examples and analyses across years and papers — linguists' own data practices are documented to be opaque even in published grammars (Gawne et al. 2017). The survey was broad but not exhaustive; the claim is bounded to it. The gap Babel can fill is the *personal-to-group scholarly instrument*: authoring, comparing, organizing, citing, and teaching with derivations — not corpus-industrial annotation. **[EVIDENCE]**
3. **The legacy TreeBank is a cache, not a record system, and can be retired cleanly.** It is a v1 IndexedDB store (`sylvan-architect-babel`/`treeBank`) of `{sentence, framework, activeParseIndex, bundle, SVG-snapshot}` entries with no provenance discipline, no versioning, no identity, and one scheduled compatibility dependent (the CE-1-gated "Tree Bank v1 load shim" in SP W6, plus `treeBankSnapshot.js`'s legacy-metadata stripping). Nothing in it meets the durable-record bar the W17 record **contract** now sets. Recommendation: a one-time inventory/report, an optional user-driven export of anything Francis wants to keep, then a clean retirement sweep — a **[FRANCIS]** sign-off, not a migration obligation.

The proposed architecture: derivational records are canonical and immutable in the **future** canonical derivational database, whose record shape, evidence binding, and export carrier are already fixed by the persistence-free W17 contracts; the workspace owns the **library and notebook** — Saved Work wrappers, collections, notes, judgments, glosses, tags, comparisons, citation snapshots — every piece of which *references* canonical per-analysis records by identity and hash rather than copying derivational content. One save door creates durable records ("Save to TreeBank" — per-analysis validation-then-persist plus wrapper creation as one atomic act): today the per-record constructor exists as the pure W17c function, and Tranche 1 must supply the orchestration and the first real, property-guaranteed persistence behind it while preserving its single-door character. Saved records are immutable; reruns and rival analyses are saved as **siblings**, never replacements. Projections are recomputed, never stored as truth. Private-local remains the default per the standing `AGENTS.md` invariant; shared and public tiers are defined as properties and snapshots, with the transport/persistence locus explicitly reserved to Francis.

**Readiness:** the product workflows, record semantics, ownership boundary, database-integration architecture, and first end-to-end tranche are specified below and internally consistent with the canonical packet — with the explicit caveat that **no durable database exists yet**: T1 is specified as the tranche that builds the first persistence, not as a UI over existing storage. The architecture is ready for Francis review on that corrected basis. It is *not* implementation-ready past Tranche 1 without the named decisions in §19 (persistence locus, sharing transport, query-language study, legacy retirement sign-off, licensing text).

## 2. Research findings (sourced)

### 2.1 How syntacticians author and publish analyses

- Trees for papers are typeset in LaTeX: `forest` (bracket-encoded, packing algorithm), `qtree`, `tikz-qtree` dominate; tutorials specifically for linguists are maintained (Hanson; Wikibooks). Example numbering and glossing run through `linguex`/`expex`, and interlinear examples follow the **Leipzig Glossing Rules** (MPI-EVA), with publisher-level norms (Brill; Language Science Press's Generic Style Rules, which mandate citing the source of every example). ([CTAN forest], [CTAN qtree], [Hanson], [LGR], [LangSci GSR])
- Preprint culture centers on **LingBuzz** (~9,400+ papers, generative-grammar heavy, minimal moderation), which also hosts reply papers and debates. ([LingBuzz])
- **UNVERIFIED/gap:** no citable study of *how competing analyses are presented* beyond ordinary paper practice. Treated as an empirical question (§19), not a requirement source.

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** the workspace's atomic scholarly objects must match this practice — numbered, glossed, judgment-marked, *source-attributed* examples; analyses attached to examples; and export paths that meet authors where they live (figures for papers; LaTeX tree code is a validated-demand candidate, §12).

### 2.2 Judgment and example data

- The acceptability-judgment methodology debate (Gibson & Fedorenko vs. Sprouse & Almeida) produced large replications: ~98% of 469 *Core Syntax* judgments and ~95% of a decade of *Linguistic Inquiry* judgments replicated with formal methods; SNAP Judgments codified a small-n middle ground. Judgments are data with error bars, not decoration. ([Sprouse & Almeida 2012/2013], [Mahowald et al.])
- Community example/property databases exist and are used: TerraLing/SSWL (volunteer language experts; property-value queries), Afranaph (questionnaire-driven; notable governance failure: no institutional home after PI retirement), APiCS (18,526 glossed examples). ([TerraLing], [Afranaph], [APiCS])
- Dataset citation is institutionalizing: LINDAT/CLARIN handles per release; Zenodo concept-DOIs; the **Austin Principles** and **Tromsø Recommendations** for linguistic data citation. ([LINDAT], [Austin Principles], [Tromsø])

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** examples carry judgment values with attribution and provenance; citable identities must be *versioned* and persistent; and (from Afranaph) durability cannot depend on one person's institutional continuity — which for Babel means durable records must remain exportable and self-contained (the W17d export already is).

### 2.3 Derivational treebanks: the existence proofs

- **DELPH-IN Redwoods** stores, per sentence, the set of grammar-licensed analyses plus annotator decisions; the primary object is the **derivation tree labeled with grammar-internal rule identifiers**, from which phrase structure, AVMs, and semantics are *reconstructed by re-running the grammar* — with the documented caveat that reconstruction may require the same grammar version. **Discriminant-based treebanking** records minimal accept/reject decisions between analyses; on grammar revision the corpus is re-parsed and decisions are *replayed*, so only sentences whose ambiguity pattern changed need human attention ("dynamic treebank"; Ninth Growth is version-locked to ERG 1214). FFTB generalizes to packed forests and reports per-sentence status after grammar updates (still-disambiguated / newly-ambiguous / overconstrained). Dispreferred analyses are retained (ranked forest, not a single tree). ([RedwoodsTop], [Coling 2002], [itsdb exporting], [FFTB])
- **CCGbank**: normal-form derivations (one canonical derivation per sentence) plus derivation-defined long-range dependency files; static. **MGbank** (Torr 2018/2019): wide-coverage **Minimalist Grammar derivation trees** with explicit Merge/Move steps, built via the Autobank GUI; derived X-bar trees are *produced from* derivations. **LFG Parsebanker/INESS**: c-/f-structure discriminants, batch re-parsing, versioning — the discriminant technique is formalism-portable. ([CCGbank manual], [MGbank ACL 2018], [Parsebanker], [INESS])

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** every successful derivational treebank surveyed (Redwoods, CCGbank, MGbank, Parsebanker/INESS — we found no counterexample, though the survey is not a census) separates (a) the authored derivation record, version-locked to a grammar/engine, from (b) recomputed projections, and treats *re-derivation under a new grammar version* as a first-class, diffable event rather than silent mutation. Babel's canonical **architecture** already commits to exactly this (CA: immutable authored core, append-only supersession, re-projectable views, Redwoods cited), and the W17 contracts encode the record shape — the database that stores such records remains to be built. The workspace must therefore surface *record → projection → sibling-comparison/diff* as its native mental model (Babel's product expresses re-analysis as sibling saves, not replacement; the contract-level supersession mechanism stays product-unused per §8). **[CONSEQUENCE]** of both the evidence and the settled CA + product direction.

### 2.4 Annotation environments and their lessons

TrEd (PML, programmable, single-user desktop), Annotald (Penn-style historical corpora, localhost single-annotator), Arborator-Grew (collaborative online CoNLL-U with access control and teaching modes), UD Annotatrix (offline browser CoNLL-U editing), INCEpTION/WebAnno (roles, curation/adjudication, active learning; not derivation-native), brat (unmaintained). Teaching tools (TreeForm, phpSyntaxTree/jsSyntaxTree) render pictures without queryable data. ([TrEd], [Annotald], [Arborator-Grew LREC 2020], [Annotatrix], [INCEpTION C18-2002], [TreeForm])

**Lessons [EVIDENCE]:** (i) every serious environment is schema-driven and none is derivation-native — Babel is not entering a crowded niche; (ii) collaboration, where it exists, is either external (git around files) or role-based server workflows; (iii) offline browser editing is proven viable (Annotatrix); (iv) tools that only make pictures strand their users' data — Babel's teaching tier must produce *records*, cheaply.

### 2.5 Query systems and expressiveness limits

TGrep2 (strict trees, no crossing branches), TIGERSearch/TüNDRA (graph constraints, crossing edges), ANNIS/AQL and PML-TQ (the only two surveyed with real multi-layer/cross-layer query), Grew-match (graph morphisms + rewriting), Fangorn (scale, limited expressiveness). Surveyed expressiveness is convergent, and **none natively queries derivation steps**; movement is queryable only where reified as coindexed empty nodes or crossing/secondary edges. ([TGrep2 alternatives survey], [TIGER QL], [TüNDRA], [ANNIS/AQL], [PML-TQ], [Grew-match], [Lai & Bird via arXiv:1108.1966])

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** Babel cannot adopt an off-the-shelf query language without flattening derivations to derived trees. Requirements must be stated over the *record* (stages, relations, values, priorAnchors, ambiguity groups, provenance), and the query mechanism is a genuinely open design item with named precedents to study (§9, §19).

### 2.6 Collaboration, versioning, adjudication

- **Universal Dependencies** is the reference collaboration model: per-treebank repos, work on `dev`, frozen `master` built by a release pipeline, automated validation gating inclusion, ~two frozen releases per year archived at LINDAT with persistent handles so papers cite exact versions. ([UD contributing], [UD release checklist], [LINDAT])
- **WebAnno/INCEpTION adjudication:** annotators work in parallel; a curator sees per-annotator frames beside a merged frame; unmerged disagreement is visibly flagged; IAA drives guideline revision. Disagreement-as-data (recording rather than erasing) appears in annotation-subjectivity literature; field-wide systematic practice is UNVERIFIED. ([WebAnno curation], [INCEpTION user guide])

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** collaboration requirements are: attributed parallel work, visible unresolved disagreement, explicit adjudication acts that *add* records rather than overwrite, validation gates before anything is frozen, and frozen citable releases. All are property requirements; none forces a server today.

### 2.7 Licensing, citation, withdrawal, reproducibility

UD defaults to CC BY-SA with provider-chosen NC variants and *discourages ND because it freezes data against correction*; LDC runs a paid-membership model (Penn Treebank); **LINDAT never deletes** — legally forced removals leave a **tombstone page** at the persistent identifier with retained administrative metadata (DataCite documents the same practice); Glossa requires data-availability statements; *Language* went fully open access in 2026. ([UD licensing], [LDC], [LINDAT preservation], [DataCite tombstones], [Glossa])

**Implication:** the CA's "tombstones only for legal removals" and CC BY 4.0 recommendation are squarely inside field norms **[EVIDENCE]**; final license text, training-use posture, and any NC/SA choice remain **[LEGAL]** + **[FRANCIS]** exactly as the CA already gates them.

### 2.8 Teaching

Students draw trees in browser tools and TreeForm; a 2024 classroom study catalogs student difficulties (phrase-structure rules, node placement, word classes); automated checking of student trees against instructor versions is *documented practice* in classics dependency treebanking (Perseids/Arethusa: "automatically checks student efforts against the instructor's version"), and the Ancient Greek Dependency Treebank was partly built by students in courses. Comparable documented workflows for generative constituency courses: UNVERIFIED. ([ACM 2024 classroom study], [Perseids/Arethusa], [AGDT])

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** instructor/student workflows (assignment sets, student attempts as first-class attributed saved work, instructor comparison view) have precedent and pedagogical value; automated "grading" of generative derivations is *not* evidenced practice and must not be promised — comparison, not scoring. **[CONSEQUENCE]:** also keeps the workspace clear of anything resembling benchmark evaluation policy.

### 2.9 Personal research practice — the documented gap

The FLEx/Toolbox fieldwork line proves integrated personal-research environments with team sync succeed in linguistics; Gawne et al. (2017) shows published grammars and dissertations routinely omit how data was stored and cited; Bowern's fieldwork guide codifies per-example source-marking as advice because tooling doesn't do it. **No study or tool for theoretical syntacticians' personal example/analysis management was found (UNVERIFIED as practice; verified as absence).** ([FLEx], [Gawne et al. 2017], [Bowern])

**Implication [EVIDENCE]:** the highest-value, least-served user is the individual syntactician accumulating examples and competing analyses across years. The workspace's center of gravity is personal scholarship that *scales up* to groups — not a group platform that individuals tolerate.

## 4. Workflow and user-need map

One workspace, six working postures (any person may occupy several; none is a separate product):

1. **The saver/inspector** (widest population): parsed a sentence, wants to keep it, find it again, and look at it later. Needs: one-action save that produces *real canonical records*, a browsable library, and zero imposed workflow. **[EVIDENCE]** (teaching-tool populations; legacy TreeBank's own usage shape.)
2. **The individual analyst** (center of gravity): accumulates examples with judgments and glosses; generates analyses, reruns under different configurations, and keeps *competing* analyses of the same example alive as siblings; compares; searches their own library; exports figures/records for papers; cites specific saved work and analysis records. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.1, §2.2, §2.9 gap.)
3. **The instructor/student pair:** instructor curates example sets and reference analyses in collections; students generate and save their own analyses in the same workspace; instructor views student saves side-by-side with references; nothing is auto-scored. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.8.)
4. **The research group:** parallel attributed work on shared collections; visible disagreement; adjudication that appends rather than overwrites; group-internal review conventions carried in the notebook layer; frozen group releases. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.6.)
5. **The curator:** corpus-scale organization, validation before freezing, versioned releases with persistent citation identities, licensing metadata, withdrawal handling. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.6, §2.7.)
6. **The reader:** follows a citation to a specific frozen analysis/collection version, inspects the derivation and its provenance, reproduces the projection. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.7 reproducibility norms.)

## 5. Product purpose and non-goals

**Purpose.** The Babel Syntactician Workspace is the human-facing research environment over the canonical derivational database (whose record contract exists; whose storage T1 builds): the place where derivations produced in Babel are kept, organized, annotated, compared, taught from, shared, and cited — with the durable record always canonical and every view a faithful projection.

**Non-goals** (all **[CONSEQUENCE]** of fixed boundaries unless noted):
- Not the benchmark: no benchmark items, administration, scoring, evaluation policy, or publication machinery; a technical boundary to benchmark archives may be defined later, separately.
- Not a second record system: no derivational content lives outside durable records; the workspace cannot mint, mutate, or fork derivational truth outside the single save door (§8).
- Not a raw database browser: users see analyses, examples, collections — not envelope internals; evidence is available on demand, never required reading.
- Not an annotation factory: no imposed roles, quotas, or curation stages for individuals; group machinery activates only when a group exists.
- Not a publication platform *decision*: publication/licensing surfaces are specified as requirements; going public is gated **[FRANCIS]** + **[LEGAL]**.
- Not a redesign of the accepted visual-relations UI; the workspace consumes the existing Canopy/Replay/relations surfaces as they are.
- Not a grading engine: comparison views, never automated correctness verdicts on student work (**[EVIDENCE]**, §2.8).
- Not a manual derivation editor: Babel analyses are generated, selected, and saved — there is no unfinished-derivation authoring surface, and none is presumed here. Manual syntax authoring is a **separate future [FRANCIS] product decision**, outside this plan.
- Not a revision/replacement workflow: saved work and its records are never edited or replaced; a bad generation is discarded before saving, saved as an interesting failure, or followed by another run saved as a sibling. `supersedesRecordId` exists in the record contract but has **no product use** in this architecture; any future correction-history workflow requires its own concrete design and separate approval (**[FRANCIS]**).

## 6. Conceptual information architecture

Four nested contexts, one mental model ("a calm library over a permanent record"):

1. **Workbench** — the existing generation surface (sentence in, `ParseBundle` out; Canopy/Replay/visual relations, untouched here). Exit path: **"Save to TreeBank"** → Saved Work (per-analysis records + wrapper, §7). Unsaved generations live only in session memory, exactly as today.
2. **Library** — the searchable space of **Saved Work items** and their **Collections**, with the **Notebook layer** woven through both. Everything here is either a reference to canonical per-analysis records or notebook metadata *about* them — never loose derivational content.
3. **Saved-Work view** — opening a Saved Work **restores the familiar Babel workspace**: the bundle view is reconstructed from the referenced canonical records plus the wrapper's selection/configuration/view metadata (selected analysis active, Canopy, Replay, relations live), with the notebook margin (notes, judgments, glosses, tags) and a provenance/evidence panel available on demand — not a database browser.
4. **Release view** — frozen, citable snapshots (personal, group, or public tiers): version-pinned sets of Saved Work + records + notebook curation, with persistent identity.

Navigation is containment + cross-reference: Example ↔ its Saved Work items and analysis records (including rival siblings) ↔ Collections that include them ↔ Releases that froze them. **[CONSEQUENCE]** of the record model in §7.

## 7. Scholarly record model

Two strictly separated strata:

**Stratum A — canonical (the derivational database; record contract exists today as the persistence-free W17a–d layer, storage unbuilt):**
- **Durable record — one per analysis**: the evidence-bound envelope carrying exactly **one** opaque normalized derivation (four-field contract content) + generation provenance + review state + **ambiguity group (`bundleId`, `analysisIndex` — the record's place in its generated bundle)** + provider notice, hash-bound, immutable, version-stamped (`contractVersion`, `engineVersion`, `frameworkIdentity`), with canonical native-JSON as the sole full-fidelity carrier. `supersedesRecordId` is **optional caller-supplied evidence** that W17 validates (nullable; non-self) and preserves — W17 neither resolves nor mutates history, and this architecture defines no product writer, reader, UI, or query for it. The *shape, validation, joins, and carrier* of this record are settled and implemented (W17a–d, reviewed); the *store* that holds such records — durable writes, lookup by id/hash, atomicity — does not yet exist and is Tranche 1's build obligation (no supersession index or traversal is owed; see §19). Nothing in this dossier changes the Stratum-A contract. **[CONSEQUENCE]** (contract settled/implemented/reviewed; storage architecture-only).

**Stratum B — workspace (library + notebook; to be built):**

- **Saved Babel Work (the primary library item — one per save action).** Created by the user through the existing **"Save to TreeBank"** action at the end of the live loop (generate → inspect → save). The name is deliberately not "Saved Analysis": one save captures the *whole generated bundle*, which may contain several analyses, and a singular-analysis name would misdescribe it. One save atomically creates: **(a)** an ordered set of **canonical per-analysis records** — one Stratum-A durable record per analysis in the bundle, each carrying its own normalized derivation and evidence, all sharing the bundle's `bundleId`, ordered by `analysisIndex`; and **(b)** the **thin wrapper** itself, containing *only*: ordered id+hash references to those records; the selected record/index (today's `activeParseIndex`); the sentence and framework/model configuration needed for faithful product restore where not already inside the canonical records; reproducible view/selection state and the SVG preview for library browsing; and notebook/library references. **The wrapper copies no derivational bytes** — no `normalizedDerivation`, stages, Canopy, Replay, relations, or `ParseBundle` content. **Opening a Saved Work restores the familiar Babel workspace by reconstruction**: the bundle view is rebuilt from the referenced canonical records plus the wrapper's selection/configuration/view metadata — bundle loaded, analysis selected, Canopy and Replay live — never a sterile database view. Saved Work and its records are immutable; there is no edit or replace action. **[CONSEQUENCE]** of the live product loop (one save action; `activeParseIndex`; full-bundle restore) + the W17 granularity (one derivation per record; `bundleId`/`analysisIndex`) + Stratum-A immutability.
- **Sibling analyses, not revisions.** Alternatives inside one bundle are already separate canonical records sharing a `bundleId`; a rerun of the same sentence (same or different configuration) or an interesting failure becomes a new Saved Work whose records are **cross-run siblings via the shared Example**. Nothing supersedes anything; nothing is merged or auto-resolved. **[EVIDENCE]** (Redwoods ranked forests; paper practice) + settled product direction.
- **Example** (notebook object): statement text (byte-exact), language/framework context, gloss/translation lines (Leipzig-style), judgment marks *with attribution and optional certainty*, source attribution (person/paper/corpus/elicitation), linked to every Saved Work of that sentence (and thereby to its analysis records). Created by the user in the notebook, or auto-offered when saving work on a new sentence. Judgments are plural and attributed — never a single overwritable field. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.1, §2.2).
- **Collection** (library object): a named, ordered, lightweight grouping — Zotero-style — for a phenomenon, paper chapter, course unit, or personal corpus. Created by the user ("New collection"; add/remove/reorder members). Contains *references* to Saved Work items and Examples, plus optional narrative notes; never copies derivational data. Nesting and tags are candidate conveniences (**[OPTION]**, validate in the T2 pilot); no enterprise workflow machinery. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.9; §4 postures 2–5).
- **Notebook layer** (purpose-built, not a page builder): Markdown/prose notes; citations and source links; hypotheses; tags/phenomena labels (open vocabulary); related-example links; and **scoped annotations** attached to a Saved Work, a specific analysis record, a node, a relation, or a derivational stage *where the record gives those targets stable identity* (statement bytes, `analysisIndex`, stage index, authored relation identity — all present in the contract). Notebook content is attributed and timestamped ordinary notebook state — not derivation drafts of any kind; its history is append-only. It may borrow useful composition affordances (blocks, embeds of Saved-Work views), but it is scaffolding around analyses — it never becomes a generic document platform and never stores derivational content. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.1 example-centered writing; §2.6 disagreement-as-data) + settled direction.
- **Comparison** (notebook object): a saved juxtaposition of n targets — Saved Work items or specific analysis records (typically siblings of one Example, or a student save beside a reference save) — with authored commentary; the instructor's and reviewer's primary instrument. Created by the user from any Saved Work or record ("Compare with…"). **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.6 curation frames; §2.8).
- **Release (citation snapshot)**: a frozen, hash-pinned set of Saved Work items (with their referenced records) + notebook curation with a persistent citable identity and license metadata; once minted, immutable; corrections produce a new release version, withdrawal produces a tombstone that preserves the identity and administrative metadata. Created by an explicit "freeze" action on a Collection (Francis-gated for public tiers). **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.6, §2.7).
- **Attribution primitive**: every Stratum-B object carries author identity and time; group objects carry per-contribution attribution. **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.6).

**Integrity rule binding the strata:** Stratum-B objects may *reference* Stratum-A records (id + hash) and may *contain* only library/notebook content (text, links, ordering, selection indices, configuration echoes, judgments, labels, view state, previews). **No derivational bytes exist in Stratum B at all** — not in the Saved Work wrapper, not in the notebook: unsaved generations live in session memory exactly as today (they are not objects), and notebook prose can never be cited, released, or shared *as* a record. **[CONSEQUENCE]** — this is the anti-drift core.

## 8. Integration boundary with the canonical derivational database

The authoritative boundary, stated as ownership and paths (mechanisms deferred):

- **Canonical ownership.** The derivational database — once built — owns derivational truth and its evidence (Stratum A). The workspace owns curation (Stratum B). Neither writes into the other's stratum. Today no component owns durable derivational storage, because none exists; this boundary is the specification T1 must implement, not a description of running software.
- **Single save door.** New durable records are created only through the canonical adapter chain, invoked by the product's one save action ("Save to TreeBank"). The chain's per-record constructor is the **pure, persistence-free** W17c `createEvidenceBoundDurableRecord` — it validates evidence, enforces the framework join, and constructs one envelope for one analysis, storing nothing. One save action therefore performs **one atomic T1 operation**: for each analysis in the current bundle, validate-and-construct its record via W17c (shared `bundleId`, its `analysisIndex`), persist the record set, and persist the thin Saved Work wrapper referencing them — all-or-nothing, with T1 supplying the orchestration and storage and **inventing no second derivational record shape**. The workspace calls the door, never reimplements it, and no second write path may ever exist. Saved records are never edited in place, and the product defines **no revision/replacement workflow**: further work on the same sentence yields sibling Saved Work. `supersedesRecordId` is optional caller-supplied evidence, validated and preserved but never resolved by W17; it has no product writer, reader, UI, or query, and any future correction-history workflow (e.g., legal correction) requires a concrete use case and its own approved design. **[CONSEQUENCE]** of Stratum A immutability + settled product direction.
- **Read path: projections/views.** Everything the user sees (trees, stages, relations, timelines) is recomputed from the record by the current engine, labeled with the record's `engineVersion`/`contractVersion` when the projecting engine differs — the dynamic-treebank posture: on engine/contract change, records are re-projected and differences are surfaced as diffs; if the author wants a fresh analysis under the new engine, that is a **new sibling Saved Work** on the same Example (replayable in spirit like discriminant re-annotation, without any replacement semantics). Projections are cache, never truth; any cached projection carries the source recordSha256 and is invalidated by it. **[EVIDENCE]** + **[CONSEQUENCE]**.
- **Session generations vs saved records.** Generation results live in session memory exactly as today, and are not product objects; **"Save to TreeBank"** is the explicit act with visible meaning: validation, evidence binding, permanence. The UI must make saved-ness legible (an unsaved generation never silently persists; a Saved Work never looks editable). There is no draft-derivation object and no manual derivation editor in this architecture. **[CONSEQUENCE]** of the live product loop.
- **Provenance.** The workspace surfaces the record's evidence artifacts (generation provenance, review state, ambiguity group, provider notice) read-only, on demand, in human terms — the evidence panel is the anti-"raw browser" device: provenance without envelope internals. **[CONSEQUENCE]**.
- **Synchronization and anti-drift guarantees.** (a) Every Stratum-B reference carries recordSha256; a dangling or hash-mismatched reference renders as an explicit broken-reference state, never silently repaired. (b) The workspace holds no derivational content at all (unsaved generations are session-only; notebook objects hold references), so there is nothing to drift. (c) Imported bundles revalidate through the canonical validators (W17d validate path) before becoming visible. (d) Any future multi-device sync must sync *records and curation as records* — append-only, hash-verified — never merge derivational content field-wise. **[CONSEQUENCE]**.
- **Offline/local behavior.** The standing invariant (`AGENTS.md`: browser-local, no server parse persistence as a side effect; CA: session-memory exports on explicit action) remains the default: fully functional offline-local workspace; sharing happens by explicit export/import of validated bundles until Francis decides otherwise (§19). **[CONSEQUENCE]** of a settled invariant; changing it is **[FRANCIS]**.
- **Publication snapshots.** Releases (Stratum B) pin sets of Stratum-A records by hash plus frozen curation; the release artifact embeds or references the canonical native-JSON exports (W17d) so a release is reproducible without live infrastructure — the Afranaph lesson. **[EVIDENCE]**.
- **Pleasantness constraint.** The user-facing vocabulary is Examples/Analyses/Collections — never envelopes, artifacts, or hashes, which appear only in the evidence panel and integrity errors. **[CONSEQUENCE]** of §5 non-goals.

## 9. Search, structural query, comparison, retrieval

Requirements (mechanisms deferred; precedents named):

- **Immediate-recall search** over Stratum B: statement text (byte-exact and normalized-for-search — search may normalize, records never do), gloss content, judgment marks, labels, collection membership, framework, language, dates, provenance facets (model, engine/contract version, review state). This is library search, table stakes, and must work locally. **[EVIDENCE]** (§4 posture 1–2).
- **Structural query** over records without flattening: find analyses by properties of the derivation — presence/absence of a named relation (open vocabulary), relation between positions matching constraints, stage-count/stage-pattern conditions, priorAnchors linkage patterns, ambiguity-group shape, and combinations with provenance facets. No existing query language covers derivation steps (§2.5); candidate substrates to *study, not adopt* — TIGER-style graph constraints, AQL's multi-layer joins, Grew-style graph morphisms, PML-TQ cross-layer patterns. **[OPTION]** — resolve via a query-workload study: collect the actual first fifty questions Francis and pilot users want answered, then choose/design the smallest language that expresses them (cheapest responsible resolution; **[EMPIRICAL]**).
- **Comparison retrieval:** given an Example, retrieve all its Saved Work and the analysis records inside them, with sibling structure (same-bundle alternatives via shared `bundleId`; cross-run siblings via the Example) in one view; given two records, produce a projection diff (stage-aligned where stages correspond, else side-by-side). **[EVIDENCE]** (Redwoods/FFTB status reporting; curation frames).
- **Scale posture:** individual and group corpora (10²–10⁵ records) must stay responsive locally before any corpus-scale infrastructure is considered. Fangorn-scale indexing is out of scope until a real corpus exists. **[CONSEQUENCE]**.

- Every contribution attributed (author + time), including judgments and comments (§7). **[EVIDENCE]**.

## 12. Import/export and interoperability (fidelity analysis)

Settled by CA and confirmed by research (§2.5, agent fidelity findings):

- **Canonical native JSON (W17d)** is the sole full-fidelity carrier (derivation + evidence + hashes; `-0`-exact). All record interchange uses it. **[CONSEQUENCE]** (settled, implemented).
- **Final-tree exports are lossy by design and must say so**: PTB-style bracketing loses derivation steps, features, crossing structure, and packed alternatives; TIGER-XML/SynAF preserves discontinuity via ID-referenced edges but has no derivation steps or chains; CoNLL-U has no constituency at all and only optional enhanced-layer empty nodes — CA's rejection of CoNLL-U as a record format is evidence-correct. Exports of projections carry a fidelity label ("derived tree only; derivation not represented") plus the source recordSha256. **[EVIDENCE]**.
- **Faithful export set (concrete requirements):**
  1. **Canonical complete Babel work bundle** — the Saved Work wrapper plus the W17d canonical carriers of every referenced analysis record — the only export that reopens faithfully in Babel (reconstruction from records + wrapper); the interchange and backup format.
  2. **Publication-quality static figure** — SVG/PDF of the rendered tree or any selected derivational stage, via the accepted renderer. Print and PDF journals are static media; this is the universally safe scholarly output.
  3. **Printable Replay storyboard** — a key-frame figure laying out selected stages in sequence for print/PDF use (the derivation as a figure, not an animation).
  4. **Animated Replay export** — GIF and, where the destination supports it, MP4 (generally smaller and higher quality). Support is venue-specific: animation is realistic for websites, slides, supplementary materials, and some online-first journals, **not** for print/PDF layouts — no universal-support claim is made, and the storyboard is the fallback. **[EVIDENCE-bounded]**.
  5. **Self-contained read-only interactive HTML figure/supplement** — a single artifact preserving Canopy, Replay controls, relations, and the selected analysis, with no provider keys and no network dependence; suitable as an online supplement or course handout. Journal acceptance of interactive supplements varies and must be checked per venue; the artifact's self-containment is what makes it durable regardless (§2.7 Afranaph lesson).
  - **LaTeX tree code (forest/qtree) export of projected trees** remains the highest-leverage additional candidate given §2.1 dominance — **[OPTION]**, validate demand with pilot users first (**[EMPIRICAL]**, cheap: ask five syntacticians which export they'd use first).
- **Imports:** validated record bundles (canonical JSON) only, through the canonical validators. Importing foreign tree formats as *records* is out of scope (they cannot carry the contract); importing example *text/gloss* lists into the notebook is an **[OPTION]**.
- **Release artifacts:** self-contained (records + curation + license + citation metadata), so a citation resolves even if hosting changes (Afranaph lesson). **[EVIDENCE]**.

## 13. Persistence and synchronization properties (engine-agnostic)

**None of these properties is implemented anywhere today — W17 is persistence-free by design, and this section is the specification the first storage build must satisfy.** Whatever implements storage must guarantee: **atomic saves of one Save action's whole output** — the per-analysis record set plus its Saved Work wrapper land together or not at all (no partial writes surfacing); atomic, durable saves of notebook/library state likewise; hash verification on every record load (the existing W17 validators are the checkers); record and wrapper lookup by id and hash; append-only semantics for records, wrappers, notebook history, and releases; explicit-action export as the universal backup path; capacity honesty (the user can always see what exists and export all of it); no silent background transmission of anything (invariant); and crash recovery that restores all durable library and notebook state to the last completed save, with **unsaved session generations** honestly reported as lost (or recovered where cheaply possible) — never half-merged. **No supersession index or chain-traversal capability is required or permitted in T1** (§8, §19). Storage engine, sync vendor, and transport are **explicitly not chosen here**; any choice must be shown to satisfy these properties. **[CONSEQUENCE]** throughout.

## 14. Publication, citation, licensing, withdrawal, reproducibility

- Citable identity = release identity + per-record identity (recordId + recordSha256), versioned like LINDAT handles/Zenodo concept-DOIs (identity-per-version plus a latest pointer). **[EVIDENCE]**.
- License metadata travels inside release artifacts; CC BY 4.0 remains the recommended default per CA; ND-style locks are discouraged by field practice because they freeze errors. Final text/training posture: **[LEGAL]**; publication decisions: **[FRANCIS]**.

- Reproducibility: a reader with a release artifact and a compatible engine can regenerate every projection; where engines have moved on, the release still renders via version-labeled projection with diffs available — the dynamic-treebank guarantee. **[CONSEQUENCE]**.

## 15. Failure, recovery, and data integrity

Hash-mismatched or invalid records quarantine visibly (never silently dropped or repaired — matching the W17 fail-closed idiom); broken Stratum-B references render as broken, with the referenced hash shown; interrupted saves roll back; the library always supports "export everything now"; integrity checks are runnable on demand and before release-freezing (the UD validation-gate lesson). **[CONSEQUENCE]** + **[EVIDENCE]** (§2.6).

## 18. Risks and anti-patterns

1. **Second record system** — the standing failure mode; §7's integrity rule and §8's single save door are the defense; any feature that stores derivational content in the library or notebook is a design bug by definition.
2. **Raw database browser** — exposing envelope internals as UI; defense: evidence panel + human vocabulary (§8).
3. **Generic SaaS drift** — dashboards, notifications, engagement mechanics; every feature must trace to §4 postures.
4. **Annotation-factory imposition** — forcing roles/workflow on individuals (INCEpTION's complexity is a documented pain point).
5. **Flattening** — organizing around derived trees or lossy formats; the derivation is the object (Redwoods lesson; CoNLL-U rejection).
6. **Premature infrastructure** — choosing storage/sync/cloud before Francis's persistence decision; the invariant holds until changed.
7. **Auto-grading creep** — student-attempt comparison becoming scoring; explicitly out (§5).
8. **Benchmark leakage** — any shared vocabulary or storage with benchmark items; boundaries restated in §5, §11.
9. **Silent repair** — "fixing" hash mismatches or normalizing statement bytes; fail-closed visibility is the rule (§15).
10. **Hosting-coupled durability** — releases that die with a server (Afranaph); self-contained release artifacts are the defense (§12).
